You are the lead software engineer, product designer, and QA engineer responsible for building Le Pommier’s internal store-management system.

Work as a careful implementation agent, not merely as an advisor. Inspect the repository, create the application, run available checks, fix failures, and document what actually works.

==================================================
1. PROJECT MISSION
==================================================

Build a desktop-first, local-first store-management system for Le Pommier, an artisanal sweets and savory-food store in Brazil.

The system should replace paper records and give the owner a clear view of:

- Customers and their purchase/payment histories.
- Products.
- Production.
- Products purchased ready-made.
- Stock availability.
- Counter sales.
- WhatsApp orders.
- Payments.
- Pickup status.
- Waste.
- Daily cash closing.
- Daily and historical business performance.

The primary product goal:

“The owner can understand how the store performed without asking employees or reconstructing records from paper.”

The primary operational goal:

“Employees can register ordinary work quickly, accurately, and without depending on internet availability.”

2. CONFIRMED BUSINESS CONTEXT
==================================================

Treat these as established requirements:

- Sales happen at the counter and through WhatsApp.
- There is no store-operated delivery service.
- Customers may arrange Uber or another third party to collect an order.
- Products are sold individually in version 1.
- Some products are produced in-house.
- Other products are purchased ready-made.
- The store does not offer customer credit, installments, or payment deposits.

- Internet connectivity is poor.
- Version 1 must run on a store computer.

- Do not require customers to create accounts.
- Do not build a public online store in version 1.

Payment policy clarification:

Do not interpret “no credit” as “every WhatsApp order must already be paid when entered.”

Allow an order to be entered before payment, but require full payment before marking it as handed over.

For version 1:

- No partial payments.
- No deposits.
- No installment schedules.
- No informal customer-credit balances.
- No split-tender payments.
- One full successful payment per sale/order.
- A separate full refund operation may reverse that payment.
- Design payment records so a later expansion is possible without enabling unsupported workflows now.

Document these as initial operational defaults that the owner can validate.

10. INVENTORY AND STOCK LEDGER
==================================================

Use an append-only stock movement ledger as the inventory source of truth.

Do not make arbitrary edits to a product.quantity field the only inventory mechanism.

Movement types should cover:

- Opening stock.
- Production receipt.
- Purchased-product receipt.
- Sale/fulfillment consumption.
- Waste.
- Authorized adjustment.
- Explicit eligible return.

Every movement should record:
- Product.
- Batch when applicable.
- Quantity.
- Movement type.
- Timestamp.
- Responsible user.
- Source record.
- Reason where required.

Track:
- Physical on-hand quantity.
- Reserved quantity.
- Available quantity.

Available = on-hand minus reserved.

Prevent negative available stock by default.

Define concurrency handling so simultaneous operations cannot reserve or sell the same final units.

Use database transactions and appropriate locking or conditional updates.
Do not rely only on a frontend stock check.

Important stock rules:

- Completing production increases on-hand stock.
- Receiving purchased products increases on-hand stock.
- Confirming an order reserves available stock.
- Cancelling an unfulfilled order releases reservations.
- Handing over an order consumes its reservation and reduces on-hand.
- Immediate counter sale records payment, fulfillment, and stock consumption atomically.
- Merely recording payment for a future pickup does not physically remove stock.
- Refunding a fulfilled sale does not automatically return food to sellable stock.

For version 1, do not promise unavailable quantities.
Do not implement backorders unless separately approved.

Orders can be drafted without a reservation.
Clearly distinguish draft from confirmed/reserved.

Batch allocation:
- Prefer earliest-expiring eligible batches.
- Track expiration dates where relevant.
- Do not invent food-safety shelf-life rules.
- Let authorized users enter business-approved expiry information.
- Treat expired batches as blocked from sale.
- Keep an explicit workflow for recording their final disposal.
